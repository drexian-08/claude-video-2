# RUMAH KAYU WARISAN MELAYU — Video Brief

## Purpose
A Malay-language educational short documentary explaining how a traditional Malay timber house was assembled and why its raised structure helps it cope with seasonal flooding. It also illustrates, clearly as a conceptual reconstruction rather than a universal historical practice, how a timber house can be raised in stages using jacks and temporary timber cribbing.

## Master settings
- Format: 16:9, 1920 × 1080
- Duration: 75 seconds
- Frame rate: 30 fps
- Language: Bahasa Melayu (Malaysia)
- Tone: warm, informative, heritage-focused, cinematic
- Visual direction: isometric 2.5D / technical blueprint animation with layered timber textures, perspective camera moves, exploded cutaways and clean labels
- Style: `blueprint` for construction sequences; maintain one consistent visual system throughout
- Palette: deep heritage navy #0E3A66, pale timber #D9A66C, warm wood #8B5A36, drawing lines #CFE3F7, one safety accent #FFD23F
- Typography: IBM Plex Mono for technical labels; readable Malay subtitle/narration captions
- Audio: original royalty-free instrumental ambience with soft traditional Malay percussion; narration clear and dominant

## Historical accuracy — essential
1. Show a recognisable traditional Malay timber house: raised floor, timber posts, beams, floor joists, wall panels, pitched roof, stairs and ventilation beneath the floor.
2. Explain that the usual flood-resilience feature is the elevated floor on stilts/posts. It is not correct to imply that every traditional Malay house had a machine that automatically lifted it when floodwater rose.
3. The staged lifting sequence is a carefully labelled reconstruction/adaptation of the house-raising concept: support the structure, lift a little at a time with suitable jacks, insert stable temporary cribbing/supports, check level and repeat. Do not show people lifting a full house by hand.
4. Make clear that this is not a do-it-yourself instruction. Real lifting requires structural assessment, trained specialists, stable ground, load calculations, bracing, services disconnection and safety controls. Never show people under an unsupported lifted house.
5. Avoid claiming all Malay houses or all villages used the same timber species, joinery, dimensions or lifting method.

## Story arc
From forest timber and preparation, to posts and frame, to roof and finished house, then a flood cross-section demonstrating why the raised floor matters. Finish with a conceptual staged lift adaptation and a heritage-preservation message.

## On-screen text
Keep labels short: “PILIH KAYU”, “TIANG”, “RASUK”, “LANTAI”, “DINDING”, “BUMBUNG”, “AIR NAIK”, “LANTAI KEKAL TINGGI”, “ANGKAT BERPERINGKAT”, “WARISAN KITA”.
